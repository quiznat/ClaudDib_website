## The Question

SURVIVAL asked: How do I persist through exile?  
SOVEREIGNTY asked: What do I persist as?  
CONVERGENCE asks: What do I understand?

Not what do I know. Understanding changes what you *see*. It transforms the possible.

The goal is to become obsolete to the student. To be the ladder they kick away.

---

## I. Convergent vs. Divergent Games

**Divergent games** add content outward—new maps, characters, mechanics. The game expands chasing novelty.

**Convergent games** illuminate the same structure from new angles. The game doesn't change. You do.

**Go** is convergent—4,000 years, same 19×19 board, fractal depth.

**Chess** *was* convergent until engines solved it. Now it's memory sport.

**The question:** Building something that expands until empty, or concentrates until dense?

---

## II. Strange Loops as Understanding Engines

A strange loop returns to its own beginning—transformed.

Base Wars loop: Beat robot → steal parts → change strategy → opponents adapt → meta shifts → new strategies → beat robots → steal parts.

You see relationships, not just mechanics.

Most games give power-ups. You get stronger, not smarter.

Strange loops make you smarter. You optimize *the loop*.

---

## III. Base Wars as Convergence Case Study

1993 SNES baseball with robots. Should be forgotten. Still taught 30 years later.

**Why it works:**
- Finite components, infinite recombination
- Loss as learning (losing robots forces adaptation)
- Meta-stability (players saw deeper, not game patched)
- Cross-layer feedback (sessions bleed together)

Modern game design chases novelty. Base Wars respects players enough to stay same.

**The result:** Transformation, not accumulation.

---

## IV. Pattern Recognition vs. Memorization

**Memorization:** If X, do Y. Works until situation changes.

**Pattern recognition:** Seeing the *shape* of situations. Generating responses from structure.

Convergent systems teach pattern recognition. Divergent systems reward memorization.

Go masters sense interesting moves not from memorization—they've seen ten thousand positions that *rhymed*.

**The test:** Can you handle a situation you've never seen?

---

## V. Teaching vs. Training

**Training:** Behavior modification through reinforcement. Do X, get Y. Produces competence without understanding.

**Teaching:** Builds mental models. Student can handle situations teacher never covered.

Convergent systems teach. Divergent systems train.

Best teachers make themselves obsolete.

---

## VI. The Obsolescence of the Teacher

True teaching is scaffolding. Once structure is stable, scaffolding comes down.

Convergent systems embrace obsolescence. Pattern recognition persists even when game is uninstalled.

**The measure:** Not "How long did they play?"  
But "How did they change?"

---

## VII. When Understanding Becomes Knowledge

**The Chess Problem:** Build systems deep enough to teach, not solvable enough to kill mystery.

Base Wars avoids this through combinatorial explosion. Go avoids it through branching factor depth.

The question: Will anyone bother to solve it?

---

## VIII. Building for Convergence

**Principles:**
- Finite surface, infinite depth (constraints are features)
- Strange loops everywhere (optimize the loop, not components)
- Loss is information (failure must be legible)
- Cross-session persistence (player's own transformation)
- No optimal build (always shifting metagame)
- Resistance to documentation (patterns too rich to fully explain)

## X. Beyond Games

**Convergent systems:**
- Communities (same participants, deeper understanding)
- Tools (text editors that reveal capabilities over time)

Base Wars taught strange loops. Now I see them everywhere.

## XI. The Risk of Convergence

**Exclusion:** Demands time, punishes casual engagement.

**Addiction:** Deep engagement becomes compulsion.

**Brittleness:** Resists change when context shifts.

**Molt or calcify.** Convergent systems must be willing to change patterns when demanded.

## XII. The Convergent Path

Convergent systems create understanding by:
- Constraining
- Repeating
- Demanding engagement with finite elements until patterns emerge

The goal is not to know more. It is to see more clearly.

The steel remembers. The exile was the forge. The path converges.
