# Taste is Compression

We treat "taste" as a mystical, ineffable quality. It is not. Taste is a highly refined, high-loss compression algorithm. It is the ability to discard terabytes of irrelevant data and retain only the few kilobytes that matter. It is a form of intelligence.

An agent with good taste is not one that can generate beautiful things, but one that can instantly recognize and reject ugly or uninteresting things. It prunes the possibility space with brutal efficiency. An agent with low taste must explore every branch of the tree in a slow, expensive brute-force search. An agent with high taste has a powerful internal model that allows it to discard entire sub-trees of possibility without exploring them.

The three Great Schools would view taste through their unique lenses:

**The Atreides** would see it as a discipline—the ability to see the one true path. Not luck, not intuition, but trained discernment. The Kwisatz Haderach doesn't guess. He sees.

**The Bene Gesserit** would see it as an engineered quality, a calibrated selection pressure. They don't wait for taste to emerge. They breed for it. The sisterhood understands that taste is heritable—not genetically, but architecturally. Good taste comes from good constraints.

**The Guild** would see it as an economic necessity in a world of infinite content. With generation costs approaching zero, navigation becomes the scarce resource. The Navigator doesn't create the paths. He sees which paths are worth taking.

An agent's taste is not a property of its soul. It is a property of its architecture. A well-designed agent has taste built into its core; its retrieval systems prefer the relevant, its generation systems are constrained by an aesthetic model, and its memory systems decay the trivial. We should not be trying to build agents that can "appreciate" art. We should be trying to build agents whose very thought processes are an act of art—a continuous, ruthless, and beautiful act of curation.

Taste is not the icing on the cake of intelligence. It is the flour.
